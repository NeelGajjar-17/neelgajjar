HOW I OPERATE

Data management is not a sequence of forms.
It is a controlled system of decisions.

Protocol interpretation affects eCRF design. Design affects validation. Validation affects live data quality. Reconciliation affects lock readiness. I approach those activities as one connected system, with traceability between the study requirement, implementation, review and final decision.

01 / LIFECYCLE OWNERSHIP

What I own across a study.

Each stage has a different output, but the quality question is always the same: can another reviewer understand what was required, what was done and why the resulting data can be trusted?

01
UNDERSTAND

Protocol Review

Translate objectives, endpoints, visits, procedures, timing and eligibility requirements into data-collection needs and identify ambiguity before build.

OUTPUTSRequirements · assessment mapping · review questions
02
DESIGN

eCRF + Data Standards

Design forms, variables, labels, response options and visit structures with CDASH considerations and downstream analysis/readiness in mind.

OUTPUTSeCRF specifications · completion guidance · standards review
03
BUILD

EDC / ePRO Configuration

Configure forms, edit checks, calculations, dynamic logic, custom queries, visit schedules and access behavior in the clinical system.

OUTPUTSConfigured database · rules · access matrix
04
VERIFY

UAT + Validation

Test protocol-to-system behavior, expected paths, boundary conditions, calculations and defects before controlled production use.

OUTPUTSDVA / UAT evidence · defects · retest decisions
05
OPERATE

Live Data Quality

Review missing, inconsistent, unexpected and out-of-window data; manage queries and recurring quality signals while the study is active.

OUTPUTSQueries · review listings · quality metrics
06
RECONCILE

Safety + External Data

Reconcile SAE, coding, central lab, ePRO and other external streams using participant, visit, timing and expected-record context.

OUTPUTSExceptions · reconciliation status · documented resolution
07
CONTROL

Documentation + Oversight

Maintain data-management plans, transfer expectations, operational guidance, access/training evidence and cross functional follow-up.

OUTPUTSDMP · DTA · guidance · oversight evidence
08
CLOSE

Database Lock Readiness

Confirm expected data, query status, reconciliation, coding, approvals and documented review are complete before controlled release and lock.

OUTPUTSReadiness evidence · approvals · release / lock documentation
02 / QUALITY CONTROL MODEL

What gets checked
before “complete” means complete.

→
DATAObserved record
→

→
LOCKDocumented state
Expectedness

Is the form, event or external record expected for this participant and visit?

Completeness

Are required data present, or is absence explained and documented?

Consistency

Do dates, times, statuses and related forms agree across the database?

Reconciliation

Do EDC and external/safety sources align, or is the discrepancy visible for review?

Traceability

Can the reviewer follow the requirement, issue, action and final decision?

Readiness

Are outstanding issues resolved or explicitly accounted for before release/lock?

03 / STUDY LEADERSHIP

The work around the data matters too.

Clinical data quality depends on decisions and coordination across study teams, sites, vendors and systems, not only on edit checks.

PRIMARY DM

Study-level decisions

Prioritize data issues, interpret study requirements, coordinate reviews and support documented sign-off and lock activities.

cross functional

Operational alignment

Work with project, clinical, site, safety, statistics, laboratory and other stakeholders to resolve data dependencies.

VENDOR / EXTERNAL DATA

Transfer + reconciliation

Define expectations, review incoming data, identify discrepancies and follow issues through resolution.

RISK / ISSUE CONTROL

Surface what matters

Use listings, metrics and targeted automation to make missing, inconsistent or delayed data visible early enough to act.
