CHAPTER 02 · CLINICAL DATA MANAGEMENT OPERATING MODEL

Clinical data is a chain of decisions.

My role is to keep that chain connected from protocol intent through system behavior, observed data, issue resolution and database lock readiness.

THE CORE QUESTION

Can another reviewer understand what the study required, what the system did, what the data showed and why the final decision was made?

01 / DECISION GATES

Four gates across the study lifecycle.

Each gate asks a different quality question and produces evidence needed by the next stage.

GATE 01BEFORE BUILD
UNDERSTAND + DESIGN

Are the study requirements clear enough to build?

I translate objectives, endpoints, visits, procedures, timing, eligibility and collection expectations into eCRF and system requirements. Ambiguity is more valuable when found here than after production.

Protocol reviewAssessment mappingeCRF designVariable and response designCDASH considerations
EVIDENCE FOR THE NEXT GATE

Review questions, agreed requirements, eCRF specifications and completion guidance.

GATE 02BEFORE GO LIVE
BUILD + VERIFY

Does the system behave the way the study requires?

Configuration is tested against expected paths and edge conditions before controlled use. The goal is not simply to confirm that a form opens, but that logic, calculations, timing and dependencies behave as intended.

EDC and ePRO configurationEdit checksDynamic logicCalculationsUAT and defect retest
EVIDENCE FOR THE NEXT GATE

Configured database, DVA and UAT evidence, defects, resolutions and retest decisions.

GATE 03DURING CONDUCT
REVIEW + RECONCILE

Is the accumulating data complete, consistent and explainable?

Live review separates what is missing from what is not expected, what is inconsistent from what is clinically or operationally plausible, and what can be checked deterministically from what requires human context.

Missing and inconsistent dataQuery managementSafety reconciliationCentral lab and external dataCoding workflowsePRO review
EVIDENCE FOR THE NEXT GATE

Queries, review listings, reconciliation status, coding status, metrics and documented resolutions.

GATE 04BEFORE LOCK
CONTROL + CLOSE

Is the database ready to become the controlled study record?

Lock readiness is the accumulated result of controlled design, validated behavior, complete review, reconciled sources, coding status, approvals and accountability for any remaining issues.

Expected data reviewOpen query reviewReconciliation completionApprovals and sign offRelease documentation
FINAL EVIDENCE

Readiness confirmation, approvals, release records and database lock documentation.

02 / HOW I REVIEW AN EXCEPTION

A discrepancy is not automatically an error.

The first task is to preserve context. I want to know whether the record was expected, whether the sources refer to the same participant and event, and whether the difference is explainable before deciding what action is appropriate.

03 / DATA LEADERSHIP

The database is only one part of the system.

Good clinical data management depends on coordination across people, systems, vendors and documented decisions.

STUDY

Own the data management thread.

Priorities, documentation, review cadence, issue follow up and readiness decisions across concurrent study work.

SYSTEM

Connect requirements to behavior.

Translate protocol intent into forms, rules, schedules, calculations, access and controlled system changes.

STAKEHOLDERS

Resolve dependencies across teams.

Coordinate with project, clinical, site, safety, statistics, laboratory and other stakeholders when data decisions cross functional boundaries.

VENDORS

Make external data reviewable.

Define transfer expectations, review incoming records, preserve source context and follow discrepancies through documented resolution.
