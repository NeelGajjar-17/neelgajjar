CHAPTER 01 · FOUNDATION

My career did not begin with data.
It began with quality.

I’m Neel Gajjar, a B.Pharm and CCDM® certified Clinical Data Manager. My path through pharmaceutical quality, clinical research and clinical data management shaped a simple professional belief: reliable data starts long before the final review.

It starts with understanding what a study is trying to collect, translating that intent carefully into systems, and keeping the reasoning behind data decisions visible.

THE THREAD THROUGH MY CAREER

Control the process.
Understand the context.
Make the decision traceable.

Those ideas connect the different stages of my background. The tools changed. The underlying discipline did not.

01 / THE JOURNEY

Each stage added a different layer of judgment.

012012 to 2018
SCIENTIFIC FOUNDATION

Pharmacy and laboratory science taught me to respect the evidence.

A Bachelor of Pharmacy at Gujarat Technological University established my pharmaceutical foundation. Chemical Laboratory Technician training at Seneca added laboratory and analytical context.

WHAT STAYED WITH ME

Measurements, methods and documentation only become useful when their context is understood.

022018 onward
QUALITY FOUNDATION

Quality work taught me that consistency has to be designed.

My early pharmaceutical quality experience strengthened my understanding of controlled processes, documentation and the importance of making work reproducible and reviewable.

WHAT STAYED WITH ME

A reliable outcome depends on the process that produced it.

032021 to 2022
CLINICAL RESEARCH

Clinical research gave the data a human and operational context.

Postgraduate training in Clinical Research, Drug Safety and Pharmacovigilance helped connect scientific requirements with study conduct, safety and the operational realities behind clinical evidence.

WHAT STAYED WITH ME

A field in a database represents a real study requirement, workflow or decision.

042022 to now
CLINICAL DATA MANAGEMENT

Clinical data became the place where those disciplines came together.

Today I work across protocol review, eCRF design, EDC and ePRO, UAT, live data review, query management, coding, safety and external data reconciliation, documentation and database lock readiness.

WHAT I APPLY NOW

Connect the requirement, system behavior, observed data and documented decision.

03 / WORKING PRINCIPLES

The standards I bring to the work.

01

Start with intent.

Understand the protocol, endpoint, workflow and user before deciding how the data should be structured.

02

Make logic explainable.

Edit checks, calculations and automation should be understandable enough to review, test and challenge.

03

Preserve source context.

Reconciliation should surface differences without silently erasing what each source actually reported.

04

Keep judgment human.

Automation can find exceptions. Clinical and operational context still determines what those exceptions mean.
